## My plans for this blog

- Blog writing also helps me thoughts off my mind to focus on other things, especially when I feel like my thoughts are so scattered everywhere.
- If I want to aim for some kind of writing goal for this blog, I want to write at least once a month. Every month I'm always doing something, or something would happen and I want to express my thoughts on it.

## Learning Japanese
- Last year I did the JLPT N5 exam, and my god was it so miserable trying to cram everything in last minute. Luckily, most of the material was stuff I already learned so I wasn't struggling too much. But I don't know what I put it off until the last few weeks to properly study it.
- This year, I want to take my time learning it as long as it's consistent. I really don't want to force my brain to learning so much in so little time, as it realistically won't work for long term memory anyways.
- If I keep it up this year, I want to aim for the JLPT N3 by December. The JLPT always runs around the first week of December, which gives me plenty of time to learn more Japanese.
- I bought a course my TokiniAndy, as I really liked his YouTube channel for reviewing Genki 1. The course included those videos, but also includes a ton of supplemental material for learning and practicing. Something that sold me was that there are a lot of videos for doing workbook practice, and Andy (as well as Yuki and Ando) would be your workbook partner in those videos. Not only this, but his community is still very active. There's a comment system for each of the lessons and practice, and there's been comments as recent as a few weeks ago with Andy responding to them as well. The course itself also wasn't a crazy price compared to other places I've seen (between $100 - $120 every year depending on what plan you buy).
- This is gonna be a tough goal to try and stay consistent for a year, but I keep going at this goal because I get a lot of satisfaction out of it. There's just something in my mind that ticks happily when I can hear or read a sentence from a native speaker, and I'm able to figure out what they're saying. This could be done with any other language, but I think Japanese seems to hit closer than others since I grew up with a ton of media that uses this language. Plus, I've studied abroad in Japan and it only made me want to master it someday after living there for about half a year.

## Finding work
- Something that I'm gonna have to be focusing on for this year is finding a full-time job. I've graduated and have still been looking for work, but also trying to find that balance where I can focus on making projects to improve my work versus hunting and applying to jobs.
- I realized I've made my work so spread out since I was trying so hard to learn as much as I could, that I couldn't figure out what to even brand myself as when I was working on my LinkedIn.
- This was a sign that I needed to specialize more so it's easier for me to find work. I always had a liking towards UI/UX design, but with how new it is I was feeling pretty intimidated of getting into it. This year though, I don't want to keep holding myself back and just go for it.
- I already have some project ideas I've been conceptualizing, and I want to put some of my interests related to these to be more than just a "cookie cutter" project I often see on UX design portfolios.
- I made my New Year's resolution to find a good job this year. If I can do that this year, I think I'll at least get a start somewhere after my education being done.

## Connecting more
- After I graduated I just felt so...empty? I got my degree, but I couldn't figure out why I felt like a husk after I left university. At first, I thought it was just because I didn't have a constant routine anymore. Thinking about it, I was doing education for most of my life. Now I don't have to do that anymore, and the transition from school to work is such a huge change.
- But even when my routine was gone and I was job hunting, I didn't seem happy. I wasn't even sure if getting a job would fix how I was feeling.
- One of the clubs back at my university needed volunteers for a "Girls in STEM" event, and I thought "why not?". I already have a love for helping young girls in STEM (or more like STEAM in my case) and took the chance to volunteer. Even though I wasn't a student anymore, I still wanted to at least give it a chance since I didn't have anything else going on.
- Volunteering for that kind of event and figuring out our plan months in advance was honestly kind of fun. It wasn't until I went on campus to help out with making posters that I realized the social aspect of my life was completely cut off after I finished school.
- Online communication with friends and colleagues didn't end after I graduated, but 
- I was so involved in clubs and activities because not only was I passionate about those things, but I also liked socially interacting with others who had similar interests as well.
- GDC 2026 is coming up this year, and (because of my work from the previous summer), I have a chance to get a convention pass and connect with other developers again like last year. One of the most intimidating things I want to do is stay at a hostel. Other than a way to save money (especially in a place like downtown San Francisco), hostels are supposed to be a way to socially interact with other travelers. With how scary it sounds since it basically is set up like a college dorm, I also hear many great things about them.
- I've taken a similar risk when I went to Japan, as I was navigating a country completely on my own. And traveling there made me realize there are people that will help you if you reach out to them, and people that will talk to you if you socialize with them. Despite not being no where near fluent in Japanese, some people will still want to talk to you.
- I think even more than ever, we need that social connection, and I want to come back to interact again even more.
