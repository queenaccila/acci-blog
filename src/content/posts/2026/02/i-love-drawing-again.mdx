## Previous struggles with drawing
From the recent past year (and probably even more than that), I was struggling to get the energy to draw and enjoy it too. Even when I had moments where I was drawing, it usually was in a situation where I "needed" to draw something. Usually this was often the case when I was making game assets.

I still was enjoying my time for drawing as a supplemental thing such as games assets, but it seemed like I was losing that feeling of just drawing for the sake of it and not "needing" to draw something.

I was at a point in my art where I felt satisfied with my art, but it just seemed so boring to draw now. My anatomy knowledge was fine, my color palettes were pleasant to look at, and I've found a lot of comfort doing cel shading as my main shading method. Even with all of this, I still didn't seem to enjoy making anything.

But then at the beginning this year, something happened-

<img src={assets.divider} className="header-img" loading="lazy"/>

## Experimenting with anatomy
If you look at my [previous post](https://blog.accila.com/posts/midnight-express-cast-updated-designs), I was testing out a new drawing style so it would be easier to animate. Even though this was a case of me needing to simplify anatomy and character designs to make it easier to animate, this type of anatomy style made something click with me.

I was doing some doodles with the Midnight Express characters, and I found it so easy and fast putting character interactions together. Next thing I knew I managed to fill in more of my abandoned sketchbooks with my characters due to drawing a lot more quickly.

<img src="https://acci-blog.b-cdn.net/2026/feb-2026/sketchbook-preview.jpg" className="main-image" loading="lazy"/>

This made me wonder if I should change my anatomy style. I realized it takes me a lot of time just to get the anatomy looking right with my previous style, and I found myself relying too much on photo references for poses for drawing any complex anatomy (nothing wrong with using references btw, it's just way too restrictive for me to draw if I have to rely on them and not use it as a supplemental resource).

## Moho and my love for animation
I could probably make this a separate post, but I wanted to lump this in with this post since it fell under the same theme (and also helped contribute to my returned love for drawing).

I bought Moho 12 when it was in a Humble Bundle sale. I usually follow Humble Bundle to get some great deals on software, and this Moho bundle made me really interested after I did some research on it.

Eventually Moho was doing an upgrade sale from 12 to 14 (the latest version). Although it was a bit of money than usual for me to spend, I eventually bought it to get the latest tools. I was so happy I trusted my gut and bought the upgrade, as the tools on 14 made such a huge difference! (Especially when Moho 14 got a free upgrade to export their rigs to game engines)

I've experimented with Moho in previous posts (which you can check it out on the [Art](https://blog.accila.com/art) section here!), and the more I work in Moho, the more I enjoy using it! Moho has so many great quality-of-life features that hits all the right spots for my personal animation workflow that I was struggling with in the past.

<img src="https://acci-blog.b-cdn.net/2026/feb-2026/ych%20demo.gif" className="main-image" loading="lazy"/>

A big factor could also be because I love doing 3D rigged animation when I use Blender, but 3D can also be pretty tedious to set up or build a model to animate later. Moho's specialty is in 2D rigged animation, and their tools are very streamlined for this type of work. It hit the sweet spot of doing bone animations, with 2D images/drawing to set it up.

Even without doing any complex animations, they make it so easy to have my drawings becoming animated without a lot of complexity (simple blinks and "hand-drawn" lines are faster to do). There's still a lot I have to learn about the program, which also makes it really exciting if I find more cool things and understand the program better!

## The community aspect
I also realized what often kept me making art was having a community there to support your work. Years ago, I used to be apart of an art community through Amino, and doing so many of the events and challenges made art so fun to do! It was probably how I managed to pump out so much art as I was doing it for people to look at, and I enjoyed making it over the years.

I have been focusing my efforts on sharing my art towards smaller communities instead of just putting my art into the void and not doing anything else. I found the quality of people viewing my work to be way better than the quantity of people who see it.

<img src={assets.divider} className="header-img" loading="lazy"/>

## Final thoughts
Overall, I would say I enjoy art and animation a lot more now because I changed things up after I was sticking with what I was familiar with for way too long. I'm really happy to enjoy drawing again!
